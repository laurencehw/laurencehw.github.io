Textbook experiments

Know the title you want? Go straight to all twenty-two books. Otherwise, find one by question, start with the five ready to teach from, or follow a reading path.

Why these exist

These are the books I wish had existed when I was in graduate school: current, free, easy to revise, and readable from the very technical to the less so. Several exist because economists should know more philosophy, history and history of thought than the curriculum asks of them; some are arguments about how a subject should be taught.

They are experiments, written with AI. The outline, the argument and the judgment of what matters are mine. Figures were generated from code using the sources identified in their captions; illustrative figures are labelled, and citations are checked against the published record. The errors that remain are mine, and if you find one, please tell me. Each book is a living draft at laurence-wilse-samson.gitbook.io with a status: course-tested, first edition, revised full draft (complete and reviewed), full draft or in progress.

Start here

If you are choosing one book to read or teach from, start with one of these five. Each has exercises with worked answers and instructor materials. Several others have more original arguments and will join the list as their exercises and reviews are completed.

Public Economics Revised full draft
For an MPP or advanced undergraduate course: theory, causal evidence and the actual rules of US programs side by side, with Rawls, Sen and Nozick on the page. The text for my NYU Wagner course. Syllabi, solutions and an assessment bank on request; the Fiscal Policy Calculator scores proposals the way its chapters describe.
Global Finance: A Money View Revised full draft
International finance as plumbing: balance sheets, dealers and the hierarchy of money, after Perry Mehrling. The first sixteen chapters are the core course; the instructor's guide has four course designs and worked solutions throughout. Balance of Power is the data companion.
Financial Economics: Claims, Prices, Holders Full draft
Corporate finance and asset pricing as one subject: the claims firms issue, the prices they trade at and the investors who hold them. Two thirteen-week syllabus maps, problems in every chapter, selected solutions public and the rest on request.
Antitrust Research Methods Course-tested
A methods manual for evidence a tribunal will accept: econometrics beside case law and agency practice in the US, EU and South Africa, every figure reproducible in R. The text for my CCRED practitioner course at the University of Johannesburg.
Economic Coercion Revised full draft
Sanctions, export controls, investment screening and tariffs as law and economics, built around a simple model of when a measure bites. Eleven tabletop exercises and worked problems; no syllabus yet, and the legal appendix awaits a specialist's review. Companion to EconWar.

Reading across the books

The books share five commitments: measurement is knowledge, balance sheets come before behaviour, the rules are part of the economy, power and distribution matter, and economies are particular to their place and history. Reading paths shows how those threads run across the collection and sets out eight combinations for courses and readers, from a first graduate year to economic statecraft.

All twenty-two, by field

Foundations

  • Institutionalist Macro: An American Approach Revised full draft — macroeconomics in the tradition of the American institutionalists, built around the national accounts and the flow of funds: what we are measuring, and how.
  • Institutional Microeconomics Revised full draft — markets, firms, and non-market arrangements as institutions for allocating resources, from the price mechanism and why firms exist through information, market design, and the commons.
  • Empirical Research Methods Full draft — organized around research questions rather than techniques, with quantitative and qualitative methods as complements, and R and Python code throughout.

Money and finance

  • Global Finance: A Money View Revised full draft — international finance as balance sheets, dealers and a hierarchy of money, where liquidity rather than solvency is the binding constraint; Balance of Power is its data companion.
  • Financial Economics: Claims, Prices, Holders Full draft — corporate finance and asset pricing taught as one subject, so that capital structure, discount rates and who owns the assets are never separated.
  • The Financial Crisis In progress — 2008 through the plumbing: the institutional arrangements and market devices that let a housing correction cascade into a global crisis, and along what lines its costs fell.

Public economics and law

  • Public Economics Revised full draft — public finance theory, causal evidence and the actual rules of US programs, with the normative questions kept in view; the text for the Wagner course, with the Fiscal Policy Calculator as its companion.
  • Antitrust Research Methods Course-tested — econometrics beside case law, qualitative evidence and agency practice across the US, EU and South Africa; the text for the CCRED practitioner course.
  • Law and Economics In progress — economic theory set beside legal doctrine, empirical evidence and practice, for readers with intermediate micro and no legal training.

Political economy and development

  • Economic Coercion Revised full draft — sanctions, export controls, investment screening and tariffs as both law and economics, in a field that otherwise has journalism and law reviews but few textbooks; companion to EconWar.
  • Political Economy Full draft — how political institutions shape economic outcomes and economic conditions shape political behavior; a comparative field text for second-year PhD students.
  • Development Economics Full draft — bridges the micro-experimental and macro-structural traditions of the field.
  • The New Population Panic Revised full draft — falling fertility as a manageable transition rather than a catastrophe: the facts are right, the alarm rests on contested philosophy.

Economies, from city to continent

  • City Economics Full draft — how cities work, economically, socially and politically, with attention to history, policy and the lived experience of urban life.
  • Regional Economics In progress — six world regions through new economic geography, institutional analysis and spatial econometrics, each section paired with an applied data lab.
  • The American Economy In progress — how each sector of the US economy actually works: who the firms are, how they are regulated, where the workers are and what the data measure.
  • The Chinese Economy Full draft — the same portrait for China, with the historical depth its transformation demands.
  • The South African Economy Revised full draft — the democratic era from 1994 to 2025 in three parts: macroeconomics and state capacity, the productive sectors, and labour, skills and distribution. Read it alongside SA Policy Space, which tracks the reform agenda the book describes.
  • The African Economy: The Growth Century First edition — Africa as the growth economy of the rest of this century, organized around the systems that cross borders (corridors, currency zones, city-regions, value chains, payment rails), with nine data tools and 54 country cards; published as a website-textbook in September 2026. Bridging African Borders tracks the cross-border reforms the book argues for.

History and ideas

  • World Economic History Full draft — from prehistory to the present, pairing each era with how we know what we know: the papers, data and identification strategies behind the evidence.
  • An Introduction to Economic Thought Revised full draft — modern questions, historical answers: every idea students now learn as settled was once contested, and the book follows the contests.
  • Philosophy of Economics Full draft — rationality, welfare, causation and scientific method, for beginning PhD students and advanced undergraduates.

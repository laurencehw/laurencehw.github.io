Tools

Interactive tools I have built, and open textbook drafts. Code is on GitHub.

Interactive

  • SA Policy Space — a research tool on South Africa's economic-reform agenda: 171 reform ideas, each traced to the parliamentary committee record that raised it and to the published research that bears on it, kept current by weekly harvests of Parliament, calls for comment, and the research literature. With a reform index, a weekly briefing, and a question tool whose answers are grounded in that evidence base.
  • The African Economy: The Growth Century — a website-textbook that treats Africa as the growth economy of the rest of this century, organized around the systems that cross borders: corridors, currency zones, city-regions, value chains, payment rails. With nine data tools and 54 country cards: a century slider for population 1950–2100, a growth-pole map, a sovereign debt tracker, an AfCFTA explorer, and a convergence calculator. First edition published September 2026.
  • EconWar — a platform for simulating events involving global economic coercive measures: sanctions, export controls, tariffs, and investment restrictions, and how targets and third countries respond.
  • Fiscal Policy Calculator — estimates the budgetary and macroeconomic effects of US tax and spending proposals from IRS and FRED data, following CBO conventions, with static, behavioral, and dynamic scoring. Source.

Open textbooks

Living drafts, published as they are written at laurence-wilse-samson.gitbook.io. The ones furthest along:

  • Economic Coercion — the tools of economic statecraft; companion to EconWar.
  • Institutionalist Macro: An American Approach — a measurement-first introduction built around the national accounts and the flow of funds.
  • Institutional Microeconomics — markets, firms, and non-market arrangements as institutions for allocating resources, from the price mechanism and why firms exist through information, market design, and the commons.
  • Public Economics — the text for the Wagner course.
  • Global Finance: A Money View and Financial Economics: Claims, Prices, Holders.
  • Empirical Research Methods and Antitrust Research Methods — the latter is the text for the CCRED course.
  • The American Economy and The Chinese Economy — sector-by-sector institutional portraits; The African Economy above is the third in the series.

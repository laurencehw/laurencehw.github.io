Tools

Research tools I have built with AI from public data. The design, the questions and the checking are mine. Each tool names the source of every number and publishes its methods; the code for the Fiscal Policy Calculator is public, and code for the others is available on request. They are at different stages, and the label beside each name says which.

Three kinds of claim appear below, and each tool keeps them apart. Measurements are published data, sourced and dated; where a chart is marked as checked, it reproduces a figure its source published. Model results, such as the calculator's scores, rest on assumptions its methods page states, and are set against a published estimate wherever one exists. Scenarios, as in EconWar, are fiction set among real institutions.

Policy design and monitoring

Fiscal Policy CalculatorBenchmarked
US tax and spending proposals, scored on CBO conventions · source

Fifty-two proposals scored from IRS and FRED data, static, behavioral and dynamic, with who pays. Every score says whether it was fitted to a published estimate or compared with one it never saw; the methods page sets 99 published scores against the model's. With a Social Security reform menu and problem sets for class.
Read with Public Economics: Chapter 17, tax incidence, and Chapter 22, the federal budget.
SA Policy SpaceLive, weekly
South Africa's economic-reform agenda, traced to Parliament and the research

Which reforms are moving, and which are stuck? Progress by package →

Each idea is traced to the committee sittings that raised it, from 4,273 meetings since 1998, and to the research that bears on it; every link to a paper is judged against a hand-labelled set, and the rejections are kept. Follow one idea, such as opening the electricity market, from Parliament to implementation, or use the reform packages, stakeholder coalitions, open calls for public comment and the weekly briefing.
Read with The South African Economy, whose reform tables, from Chapter 3 on, link each reform to its record here.
Bridging African BordersPublic draft
the policy blockers to cross-border investment in Africa, traced to the documents · launched October 2026
One blocker: South Africa's exchange-control rules date from 1961. Their replacement was published in draft in April 2026, and no final version has been found. Each reform carries a dated status on four tracks (law, rules, delivery, funding), and each citation says whether a person has checked it. Covers payments and currency, regional power trade, transport corridors and the AfCFTA. The African sibling of SA Policy Space, and the policy companion to The African Economy.

Money and financial systems

Balance of PowerIn development
the global monetary system, measured · where marked, a chart is checked against a figure its source published

When does the world run short of dollars, and who gets them from the Fed? The swap-line chart →

Charts of the dollar system in three layers: the hierarchy of money (what dollars cost and who can borrow them when they run short), the dollar's transaction cycle (pricing, financing, risk transfer, payment, reinvestment) and the statecraft toolbox of sanctions, export controls and tariffs. It measures the past and makes no forecasts. Exercises for class go with the charts.
Read with Global Finance: A Money View, Chapter 16 on swap lines, and Chapter 22 of Institutionalist Macro on the dollar system.
Monetary Policy TrackerLive
every Federal Reserve rate vote since 1987 · updated after each decision
Were FOMC dissenters onto something? Not much more than their usual lean predicts: across 107 rate dissents, 58% pushed toward the neutral rate, against 54% expected. Governors are the exception. With every vote and what each dissenter wanted, linked to the Fed's own record, and four estimates of the natural rate.
Read with Chapter 13 of Institutionalist Macro, on how the Committee votes.

Simulation and teaching

EconWarTeaching game
a simulator of economic statecraft · companion to Economic Coercion
Play South Africa's foreign minister two weeks before a BRICS summit: Washington says that if the summit adopts a reserve-currency resolution, South Africa stays off the AGOA renewal and its 30% tariff stays. Thirty-three scenarios, played from Washington, from Beijing or from a middle power that must hedge, built on the book's tabletop exercises. The scenarios are fiction set among real institutions; the advisers' dialogue is written by AI as you play. Or watch a demo first.
Read with the tabletop exercises in Economic Coercion, each of which points to a scenario to replay here.
